The Suboptimal Optimum

Most frameworks ask two questions: did Party A win? Did Party B win? If both say yes, we call it success.

But that misses the real question. Did the system win?

The market. The institution. The trust layer. The long-term viability of the environment both parties depend on. Two parties can optimize beautifully while the system beneath them degrades. That is the pattern we call the Suboptimal Optimum — locally rational, systemically destructive.

"Consumption is the sole end and purpose of all production; and the interest of the producer ought to be attended to, only so far as it may be necessary for promoting that of the consumer." — Adam Smith, Wealth of Nations, 1776

Smith granted producers legitimacy only as servants of the whole. For 250 years, we've quoted the invisible hand while ignoring this constraint. Meta was the prototype of what happens when it breaks. AI is the scale-up.

Integration at Every Scale

Paxology measures and designs for integration — outcomes where all parties and the system itself move in a positive direction together. Not compromise. Not trade-offs. Structural integration that compounds at every level.
